Master DEBRIEF: MASTER GAME DEVELOPMENT PROMPT — THE EXTRECATED
1. Project Overview
Help me plan and develop my survival-horror game, working title The Extrecated, using Godot 4.x.
The game takes place during a fictional interpretation of the Christian Second Coming, focusing on the people left on Earth after the saved are taken into Heaven. The game should emphasize psychological horror, disturbing creature designs, environmental storytelling, desperation, and the collapse of ordinary life.
The intended visual style is gritty, dark, and reminiscent of late-1990s PlayStation survival-horror games. The game should use genuine low-poly 3D environments and characters, fixed cinematic camera perspectives, intentionally low-resolution textures, unique character animations, and carefully controlled lighting.
My original visual target is described as 64×64 3D models. Interpret this as a deliberately low-resolution, retro-inspired presentation, using 64×64 textures where appropriate, rather than assuming that a 3D model itself is a 64×64 sprite.
Use Godot 4.x to create and run the game. Prioritize a manageable development workflow, reliable scene organization, clear collision geometry, and performance suitable for the intended hardware.
Do not replace the intended 3D environments with large 2D background images. Environment reference images must guide the placement and proportions of real geometry, models, props, roads, furniture, and other objects.
2. Core Premise and Lore
The Christian faith believes in the Second Coming. In this game's interpretation, the true Christians are brought into Heaven, leaving their earthly bodies behind. Demons then inhabit these abandoned bodies, using them to roam the Earth.
These demons are known collectively as the Extrecated.
The defining mark of an Extrecated creature is a cross branded or burned into some part of the possessed human body. The mark looks deeply cauterized and may appear to retain heat or emit a faint trace of smoke.
The cross is an identifying visual element, not automatically a gameplay weakness.
The demons differ in how much control they possess over their host bodies. Weaker entities struggle with ordinary human movement, stronger ones deliberately manipulate anatomy, and the highest entities can control many bodies simultaneously.
The original human appearance and the demon's actual behavior should remain distinct concepts. The creature occupies a human body, but its intelligence, intentions, and capabilities belong to the possessing entity.
Establish consistent world rules and reveal them through encounters, notes, environmental storytelling, and exploration rather than explaining everything immediately.
3. Visual Direction and Gameplay
Visual style
Gritty and dark, inspired by classic PlayStation-era survival horror.
Low-poly 3D models with restricted palettes and intentionally low-resolution textures.
Fixed cinematic cameras, with deliberate camera changes at doorways, corners, and major story events.
Heavy rain, wet surfaces, fog, oppressive darkness, harsh fluorescent lighting, and deep shadows.
Unique creature silhouettes and distinct animation sets.
Restrained effects that emphasize movement, lighting, and sound instead of constant visual noise.
Environmental geometry must be physically present in the 3D world.
Use actual floors, walls, ceilings, doors, windows, roads, cars, furniture, and obstacles. Add collision geometry so objects that appear solid behave appropriately.
Camera direction
Use fixed Camera3D perspectives with predetermined camera positions and trigger-based transitions.
Plan the environment around camera composition. Players must understand where the exits and important threats are, even when visibility is restricted.
Maintain consistent character controls across camera changes. Avoid sudden changes of movement direction caused by camera rotation.
Special camera angles may be used during boss encounters, but attack readability must take priority over spectacle.
Gameplay direction
The main game is a survival-horror experience. The exact combat system is not finalized, so do not assume that the game must use firearms, melee combat, or stealth exclusively.
Explore resource scarcity, deliberate movement, environmental interactions, threatening enemy encounters, and situations where avoiding an enemy may be preferable to fighting it.
Do not introduce elaborate combat systems before basic character movement, cameras, collision, and enemy animations are reliable.
4. Enemy Hierarchy: The Extrecated
ACROSS — Lowest tier
ACROSS creatures possess limited control over their bodies, bodily functions, and human reasoning.
Their appearance:
Extremely thin and malnourished, almost as though the body has been vacuum-sealed around its skeleton.
Pale skin with sharp ribs and vertebrae protruding through the torso and back.
Completely white eyes without visible pupils or irises.
Remnants of the human host's original hair.
A large branded cross on the lower hip or another clearly visible body area, appearing deeply cauterized and possibly still smoking.
Their movement should appear poorly coordinated, twitchy, and animalistic. They can crawl and sprint on all fours, make abrupt changes of direction, and lunge at humans.
They are common enemies that become dangerous through unpredictability, confined spaces, and numbers.
The ACROSS should not move like a conventional zombie. Its movement should look like a demon attempting to operate a human body without fully understanding how a human body works.
BCROSS — Middle tier
BCROSS entities possess significantly greater control over their host bodies. Some can manipulate particular anatomical features to enhance physical capabilities.
Potential variations include:
Brutes with enlarged arms for powerful attacks.
Runners with enlarged legs and explosive acceleration.
Reachers with elongated necks and arms for extended melee range.
Tacticians with enlarged, distorted heads and more deliberate behavior.
Bulwarks with dense, enlarged torsos and greater durability.
Contortionists with flexible, asymmetrical limbs and unusual attack angles.
These are initial design concepts. They should not all be implemented immediately.
BCROSS enemies should have medium health pools and distinct combat behaviors. Their mutations should alter the way the player approaches each encounter, not merely increase their statistics.
CCROSS — High tier
CCROSS entities have near-total control over their bodies and are highly intelligent and malicious.
They can deliberately reshape their anatomy, alter the reach or configuration of their limbs, manipulate body proportions, and use deceptive or coordinated attacks.
They are difficult to incapacitate because their movement and anatomy are not governed by normal human limitations.
Their animations should be controlled and intentional, contrasting with the erratic movement of an ACROSS. They should appear to choose their attacks rather than merely react.
CCROSS enemies are elite threats that reward observation, timing, and learning particular vulnerabilities.
Kindred — Highest tier
Kindred entities can control multiple bodies simultaneously and may combine dozens or hundreds of Extrecated bodies into enormous, grotesque amalgamations.
They can incorporate stone, religious architecture, and other materials into their forms.
Kindred bosses should be distinct encounters with multiple phases, unique animation languages, readable attacks, and substantial changes in arena conditions.
They should not simply be larger enemies with more health.
5. Kindred Boss Concepts
Living Choir
A four-legged beast whose front legs feature enormous yellow eyes emerging from the flesh.
Its legs are covered in the cracked stone of chapel walls, decorated with religious carvings and gothic architectural details. Cracks in the stone reveal red Extrecated flesh underneath.
Its upper body resembles an arachnid-like platform. Humanoid figures emerge from the flesh, forming the choir. These figures are red, possess small white eyes, and have large mouths that extend down their chests.
The Living Choir attacks physically and produces powerful sonic attacks that damage and disorient the player.
Suggested phases:
The Procession: The boss patrols a chapel or ruined plaza while the singers turn toward the player.
The First Hymn: Individual singers provide warning cues before coordinated sonic attacks. The player must find safe spaces or use cover.
Broken Harmony: Damage disrupts the choir, producing irregular sonic attacks and forcing the main body to reposition.
The Last Verse: The stone armor fractures, exposing flesh. The boss becomes more aggressive and combines physical slams with shorter, overlapping sonic attacks.
Use sound as both an attack and an atmospheric tool. Sonic effects must be readable and should not remove player control for excessive periods.
The Archangels
A massive stone behemoth depicted as three gothic angel statues fused into a single structure.
At the bottom stands Michael, holding a sword in his right hand and a large shield in his left.
Beside him stands Uriel, holding a staff in his left hand and an open book in his right.
Above them stands Gabriel, holding a large stone tablet overhead with both hands.
The statue contains a living mass of flesh underneath its stone exterior.
The encounter begins with Michael's sword suddenly striking down toward the player, catching them off guard.
Suggested phases:
The Judgment: Teach the player to recognize Michael's overhead sword strike.
The Threefold Assault: Michael uses the sword and shield, Uriel introduces ranged attacks or area denial, and Gabriel creates delayed attacks with the tablet.
The Fracture: Stone sections crack and fall away, exposing flesh, bones, and eyes.
The Unveiling: Flesh spills out of the damaged statue and develops new appendages, changing the boss's reach and attack patterns.
Each figure must have distinguishable warning animations, sound cues, and attack rhythms. The challenge should come from coordination and positioning rather than unavoidable attacks or excessive screen clutter.

11. ACROSS Encounter Animation and Sound Direction
The encounter depends on contrast between a normal birthday party and sudden chaos.
Implement a sequence controller with these states:
PARTY_NORMAL → WINDOW_IMPACT → CREATURE_LANDED → CROWD_INSPECTION → NEAR_SILENCE → ACROSS_AWAKENS → FIRST_LUNGE → PANIC_AND_ESCAPE
Each state should have a specific trigger, expected duration, animation, sound behavior, and camera behavior.
Audio stages
Normal party: Conversations, children, movement, and rain.
Impact: A sudden sharp glass crash and low impact accent, with a brief camera reaction.
After impact: Glass settling and the ordinary ambience dropping away.
Inspection: Hesitant footsteps, faint murmurs, and background rain.
Near-silence: Almost all party ambience is reduced, with only faint environmental sounds remaining.
Awakening: Sudden movement and a sharp, unsettling vocalization.
Attack: Screams, running, overturned furniture, and abrupt animalistic movement sounds.
Create a noticeable contrast without using playback levels that are painfully loud.
Animation requirements
The ACROSS must be completely still immediately before its awakening. Avoid obvious preparatory movement.
Synchronize the glass shatter, impact animation, debris, sound, camera reaction, NPC approach, ambience reduction, sudden body movement, and first lunge with animation markers or an explicit sequence controller.
For this first encounter, scripted movement is preferable to autonomous enemy AI.
Once the player begins escaping, hand control to the appropriate gameplay system.
12. Environmental Storytelling
Survivor notes and environmental details should each have a purpose: revealing events, teaching the player something about the Extrecated, or creating an emotional connection.
Note N1 — The Neighbor
The narrator describes an elderly neighbor who had been screaming and shouting inside her house. When the narrator investigates, the neighbor comes crashing through a window and attacks with abnormal strength and speed.
The narrator retreats inside, but the front door cannot stop her. The narrator becomes trapped and struggles with the idea of hurting someone they know.
Use this note to communicate that:
Extrecated entities can inhabit ordinary people regardless of their former physical condition.
An apparently weak ACROSS may be stronger than a healthy human.
Recognizing a former person can make an encounter more emotionally difficult.
Avoidance and escape may be preferable to confrontation.

Norah Augustine is a woman in her early thirties with deep brown hair, hazel eyes, soft full lips, a smaller nose, and round, full cheekbones. She is married.

15. Development Roadmap
Build the game as small, testable milestones.
Phase 1 — Foundation
Create the Godot 4.x 3D test scene, establish visual settings, and implement reliable character movement, collisions, and fixed-camera transitions.
Phase 2 — Community center
Construct the hallway, foyer, and party room from real 3D geometry. Match the supplied reference images while preserving coherent room connections.
Phase 3 — Characters
Create Kenneth, Adaline, the Stranger, Rachel, supporting partygoers, and the first ACROSS. Establish animation and collision systems.
Phase 4 — First horror encounter
Implement the complete window breach and attack sequence with scripted camera changes, sound, NPC behavior, and the transition into panic.
Phase 5 — Escape and parking lot
Connect the interior to the exterior. Build the parking lot, BMW, curb, entrance walkway, and scripted reunion with Adaline and the Stranger.
Phase 6 — Town and driving
Construct the connected roads, intersections, and surrounding buildings. Implement the drive with actual 3D scenery, a scripted vehicle path, and the Stranger's transformation.
Phase 7 — Polish
Refine rain, lighting, sound, animation, camera framing, and performance. Test the entire prologue from beginning to end.
Phase 8 — Main game
Establish Norah's story, implement BCROSS variants, develop the CCROSS system, construct the first full exploration area, and prototype a Kindred boss.
Do not build the entire town or all enemy tiers before validating the first environment and encounter.
